Why your replies matter more than your star rating

Most Chinese-Australian restaurant owners either don't reply at all, or paste the same "Thank you so much!" fifty times. Both are worse than writing nothing. A prospective customer reading your reviews — especially a Chinese-speaking one who found you on Xiaohongshu — isn't just counting stars. They're checking: does this owner sound like a real person, or a chain?

The good news: Google review replies are short. Three prompts cover 90% of the reviews you'll ever get.

The problem with generic ChatGPT replies

Here's a real review pattern (anonymised):

"Came for yum cha Saturday. Food good but waited 25 min for the trolley. Staff too busy chatting to notice. 3 stars."

Generic reply (what most people get first try): "Thank you for your feedback! We strive to improve and hope to see you again!" — This ignores the specific complaint, sounds like every other chain, and implicitly concedes the 'staff chatting' point.

Prompt-driven reply: "Hi Sarah — thanks for the honest feedback. Saturday yum cha is our peak window and a 25-minute trolley gap means we got the floor wrong that day. We've added two roving servers on Saturdays since then. If you give us another go, ask for the table near the kitchen — dim sum reaches you in under 8 minutes." This acknowledges specifically, shows action, avoids ACL exposure, sounds human.

3 prompts to copy now

Prompt 1 — 5-Star Reply
Use when: a happy customer leaves a genuine positive review.
Reply to this 5-star Google review for my [café / restaurant / takeaway] in [suburb]. Tone: warm, specific, not gushing. Mention one detail they raised. End with one dish or experience to try next time. Under 80 words. Review: "[paste review here]"
⚠️ Don't say "we're so humbled" — it sounds like corporate PR, not a local owner.
Prompt 2 — 3-Star Recovery
Use when: a customer had a mixed experience and mentions something specific.
Reply to this 3-star Google review professionally. Acknowledge the specific complaint without admitting fault. Mention one concrete improvement we've made. Invite them back with a specific reason. Under 90 words. Do NOT mention prices, refunds, staff names or food safety. Review: "[paste review here]"
⚠️ This is your highest-leverage reply — a recovered 3-star often turns into a 5-star on the next visit.
Prompt 3 — 1-Star De-escalation
Use when: an angry or accusatory review needs a calm, offline response.
Reply to this negative Google review. Stay calm and professional. Do NOT admit specific fault. Do NOT mention prices, refunds, food safety, or staff names. Invite them to email [your email] so we can resolve this properly. 50 words maximum. Review: "[paste review here]"
⚠️ Food safety or illness claims: don't use AI at all. Draft offline, have someone else review before posting.
⚠️ AU Compliance — what NOT to put in ChatGPT
  • Customer contact details (phone, email, address) — strip before pasting. Australian Privacy Principles restrict sharing identifying data with overseas processors.
  • Staff names — use "our team" or "our floor staff" instead.
  • Food safety or illness claims — never use AI to draft a reply. Handle offline and seek advice if formal.
  • Specific price commitments in writing — keep to phone or email. Written price claims can trigger ACL §29.
  • Internal disputes — keep entirely offline.

FAQ

Does ChatGPT understand Cantonese / Mandarin restaurant context?

Yes for common dishes (yum cha, dim sum, hot pot, char siu). It struggles with your specific house names. Fix: paste 5–10 of your past replies into the prompt as voice samples.

What if a review accuses us of food poisoning?

Do not use AI. Draft a holding reply offline ("We take this seriously — please email us at [email] so we can investigate") and seek advice before posting.

Can I paste a customer's email or phone into ChatGPT?

No. Strip all personal contact info before pasting. Australian Privacy Principles restrict sharing identifying customer data with overseas processors.

Do I need ChatGPT Plus?

Free tier is enough for review replies. Plus helps if you batch a week of content at once.
